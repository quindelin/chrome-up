# The Meaning Crisis — notes

- id: `11-the-meaning-crisis`
- kind: original, category: mental-health
- Rhetorical model: Alexander's speech at Opis (Arrian, *Anabasis* 7.9–7.11: the inventory of what was built together, the "go home and tell them" reversal, the reconciliation feast and prayer for concord) and Washington's Farewell Address (the sober counsel of "an old and affectionate friend").
- Word count: 16,724 (`wc -w speech.md`, including headings and cues)
- Estimated runtime at 140 wpm: about 119 minutes (1 hour 59 minutes)

## Rhetorical plan

- Opis frame: open with the mutiny and the question "what have we built together, and what do we owe?"; use the inventory form ("They found us... they gave us...") for what earlier Americans built; reverse the "go home and tell them" taunt; close on the common table of 9,000 guests and one libation.
- Washington frame: a closing section of plain counsels, in the voice of an old friend who does not expect to be fully heeded.
- Devices: anaphora ("They built..."; "We did not..."), tricolon, antithesis (freedom from / freedom for), concrete stories carrying abstract points, direct address, a build to the peroration.
- Honesty: Arrian wrote some four and a half centuries after the event and the words are his reconstruction; Alexander was a conqueror and the speech says so. Counterargument (the decline as liberation; nones who live full lives; belief remains high) is stated at full strength in section VII and answered.
