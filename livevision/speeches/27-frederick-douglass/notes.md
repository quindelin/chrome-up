## Sources

The 1892 scan has both lines.

## Editorial changes (the speeches' words were not changed)

- Speech headings, section headings, two italic context notes and a few delivery cues were added. The section headings for III and V to IX are the pamphlet's own subheadings, put in title case. The rest are editorial and use Douglass's own phrases where possible.
- Dropped: the pamphlet's "Oration." title line, the Wikisource license footer, and two printed footnotes. The 1852 footnote reads "Rev. R. R. Raymond". The 1892 footnote quotes Lincoln's letter to Hodges ("I am naturally anti-slavery..."). The brief allows no footnotes.
- The Wikisource scan of the 1852 text prints the prime character (′) for apostrophes. These became normal apostrophes.
- Two misprints in the 1892 printing were corrected, as Wikisource marks them with {{SIC}}: "matttered" became "mattered" and "powererful" became "powerful".
- A check script confirmed that the word sequence of speech.md, with headings, notes and cues removed, matches the source texts exactly (after the changes above).

## Things the reader should know

- In section XIV, the 1892 printing reads "the fourteenth day of April, 1866, of which this is the eleventh anniversary". This is a misprint for 1865: Wikisource marks it {{SIC|1866|1865}}, and other printings read 1865. It is kept verbatim. The reader may want to say "1865".
- Numbers are left as printed ("4th of July", "76 years", "1776"), not spelled out, because the words could not be changed. Read "4th" as "fourth".
- The 1852 text includes quoted verse (Longfellow, Shakespeare, Psalm 137 and others) and ends with a long poem by Garrison. Each verse line is its own short paragraph so it reads well on the HUD.

## Not included

- "Self-Made Men", which the manifest suggests. A public text exists at https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/self-made-men/, transcribed from the Library of Congress Frederick Douglass Papers (https://www.loc.gov/item/mss1187900531/). It runs about 12,000 words, or about 86 minutes. Adding it would bring the total to about 27,000 words, or about 3 hours 15 minutes, well past two hours. It is also not on the brief's list of approved source sites, so it was left out.
- Other public-domain options, for a longer version later:
  - The 1880 Elmira speech on West India Emancipation, in the same 1892 Appendix, scan pages 609 to 626. About 5,900 words, about 42 minutes.
  - The 1883 Address on the Twenty-First Anniversary of Emancipation in the District of Columbia, on Wikisource. About 7,700 words, about 55 minutes.
  - Either one would push the total to about 2.5 hours or more.
