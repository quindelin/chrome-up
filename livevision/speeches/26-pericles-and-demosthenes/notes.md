## Sources (all public domain)

1. **Pericles' Funeral Oration and Last Speech.** Thucydides, *The History of the Peloponnesian War*, Book II, translated by **Richard Crawley** (1874; Crawley died in 1893). Project Gutenberg eBook #7142.
   https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/7142 (text file: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7142/pg7142.txt)
   - Funeral Oration: from "Most of my predecessors in this place..." to "...you may depart."
   - Last Speech: from "I was not unprepared for the indignation..." to "...the greatest men and the greatest communities."
2. **Demosthenes' First, Second and Third Philippics.** *The Olynthiacs and the Philippics of Demosthenes*, "Literally Translated, with Notes" by **Charles Rann Kennedy** (Bohn's Classical Library, 19th century; Kennedy died in 1867). Project Gutenberg eBook #6878.
   https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6878 (text file: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/6878/pg6878.txt)

**Dates in the context notes** come from Wikipedia, which cites Thucydides 2.34-46 and Demosthenes: "Pericles's Funeral Oration" (winter of 431 to 430 BC, Kerameikos), "Demosthenes" (First Philippic 351 to 350 BC), "Second Philippic" (344 to 343 BC) and "Third Philippic" (341 BC). The Last Speech is dated by Thucydides' own narrative: the second summer of the war (430 BC), after the second invasion and the plague.

## Editorial handling (words unchanged)

- I extracted the text by script and checked it by machine. The word sequence in `speech.md` matches the source exactly once the translator's footnotes are removed.
- **Removed:** Kennedy's inline `[Footnote: ...]` annotations, which are his commentary and were not spoken. I also left out his "Argument" summaries and Thucydides' surrounding narrative.
- **Kept as italic bracketed lines:** Kennedy's stage directions for documents read aloud by the clerk: *[Statement of ways and means.]*, *[The letter is read.]*, *[Here the proposed answer was read]* and *[Here is read the public document...]*. The texts of these documents do not survive and are not supplied.
- **Removed:** Crawley's opening quotation marks at the start of each paragraph and his closing mark at the end of each speech, since they are typographical only.
- **Split for the HUD:** Long source paragraphs are broken into chunks of about 1 to 3 sentences. Source paragraphs that Gutenberg had split around footnotes were rejoined.
- **Kept as printed:** Original spelling and the translators' idiosyncrasies are left alone, for example "any thing", "embassadors", "the subjects of discussion is", "Greece, was formidable" and "if no social standing".
- **Added:** Headings, one italic context note per speech, and `[PAUSE]` between sections. Nothing else was added.

## Not verified or not completed

- The clerk-read documents in the First and Third Philippics, and the proposed answer in the Second Philippic, do not survive in the sources. Only the stage directions are given.
- WebSearch was not used. The texts were fetched directly from Project Gutenberg.
