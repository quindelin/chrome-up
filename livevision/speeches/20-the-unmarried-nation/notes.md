## Rhetorical model

Two models braided together.

- **Alexander at the Hyphasis** (Arrian, *Anabasis* V.25-29, Chinnock translation, 1884, public domain). The leader rallies an exhausted army by reciting what it has already crossed and conquered, insists the labours are shared, and asks for one more river. Coenus answers that the men yearn for parents, wives, children and home, and Alexander, after three days in his tent, turns back. The speech uses both halves honestly: the rallying cry, and the lesson that what tired people want most is a home and someone in it. Techniques borrowed: the litany of things already crossed, direct address to a weary audience, "the labours are common to you and me", the river as the image of one more effort.
- **FDR's fireside chats** (first chat, 12 March 1933, on the banking crisis). Intimacy and plain speech: "my friends", explaining a complicated system as you would at a kitchen table, short sentences, concrete examples, treating the listener as a partner, ending on shared responsibility. Only short fragments are referenced; no sentences copied.

## Thesis

America is not only having fewer children. It is forming fewer couples. Marriage, partnership, dating and even sex have all declined, and they have declined unevenly, so young men and young women now differ in education, politics and expectations. Since most American children are still born to married parents and almost all are born to couples, fewer couples means fewer children. The fix is not coercion or nostalgia but rebuilding the conditions under which people meet, trust, commit and can afford a home: put phones in their place, fix the boys' education gap, make family formation affordable, end marriage penalties, rebuild places to meet, and change the norms that tell the young to wait.

## What could not be verified, and how the speech handles it

- The session's web-search budget ran out partway through research, so some figures were checked only by fetching primary pages directly. Anything that could not be checked was cut or framed as opinion.
- Official US total fertility rate for 2024/2025: not confirmed from a CDC page. Speech says "about one point six" (UN figure 1.63).
- Current Census median age at first marriage: not extracted from the Census chart. Speech uses Pew's 2011 figures and says only that the age "has kept rising".
- Share of the fertility decline attributable to less marriage versus lower marital fertility: no verified decomposition. Speech argues it from verified pieces (39.5% of births unmarried, so about three in five within marriage; 8% vs 61% living with own child) and frames the rest as argument.
- The 2018 Atlantic "sex recession" article could not be fetched, so the speech does not quote it or cite its numbers. It mentions only that writers began calling it a "sex recession".
- Stanford "How Couples Meet" figures could not be fetched, so they were left out; Pew's dating-app figures are used instead.
- The speech's claim that many schools and states have moved toward phone-free school days is kept general, with no count of states. The "many young people say they would not date across party lines" line is explicitly hedged ("whatever the exact share").
- Richard Reeves's *Of Boys and Men* (2022) and its proposal to start boys in school a year later are referred to from general knowledge, as his argument, with no numbers attached.
