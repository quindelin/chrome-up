## Thesis

The decisive question of the AI economy is not whether machines will do the
work but who will own them. A five-part program (a floor, a stake, a share,
a fair ledger, a ladder) can make sure the prosperity they produce is broadly
shared, as Alaska and Norway did with oil.

## Framing choices and honesty notes

- Illustrative people in the speech (a woman in Bethel, a machinist, a young
  paralegal) are explicitly framed in the script as imagined, not reported.
- The policy design of the "American Commons Fund" and "compute dividend" is
  the speaker's own proposal, presented as opinion and argument.
- Cost of a full UBI is presented as simple arithmetic (roughly 260 million
  adults times twelve thousand dollars), not a sourced estimate.
- Norway per-person figure is arithmetic: fund value divided by a population
  of roughly 5.6 million.
- Where sources conflicted (GiveDirectly monthly amount; Canaries paper 13% vs
  16%; hyperscaler capex by company), the speech uses the primary or most
  conservative figure and says "about" or "reported".
- Kenya study: about $22.50 a month, per GiveDirectly's own page.

Universal basic income and cash trials
- OpenResearch Unconditional Cash Study, NBER Working Paper 32719, "The Employment Effects of a Guaranteed Income": https://www.nber.org/papers/w32719 (1,000 people at $1,000/month for 3 years, 2,000 controls at $50; income ex-transfers down ~$1,900/yr; participation down 4.2 points; hours down 1 to 2 per week; leisure the largest increase; no measurable job quality gain).
