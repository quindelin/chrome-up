## Thesis

The world is not going extinct. It is shrinking and aging, which is a quieter
and slower change. Below-replacement fertility now covers most of the planet
outside sub-Saharan Africa, and the UN and the Lancet/IHME forecasts both put
peak humanity within this century. The speech argues that the right response
is neither panic nor coercion. It is to keep the promise every generation makes
to the next: build a society where people can have the children they say they
want, and run the shrinking well where it is coming anyway.

## How the rhetorical models are used

- **Pericles:** the oration praises the city before it praises the dead, then
  turns to obligations owed by the living. The speech borrows that structure:
  what we inherited, what kind of society it is, what we owe. Three short
  public-domain quotations from Crawley (1874) are used and attributed: "heroes
  have the whole earth for their tomb", the line on bringing "the interests and
  apprehensions of a father" to public decisions, and the closing promise that the
  children of the fallen "will be brought up till manhood at the public expense".
  The speech says plainly where Pericles' world was narrower than ours (he spoke
  to men, about war).
- **Lincoln:** compression, antithesis and the dedication of the living ("it is
  for us the living"). Short public-domain phrases are quoted and attributed.
  Each section ends on a short, plain line. The peroration is a run of
  dedications in Lincoln's style.
- **Techniques:** anaphora ("We owe them..."), tricolon, antithesis (extinction
  vs shrinking, panic vs neglect), direct address, and concrete stories (an
  empty Korean classroom, a Japanese vacant house, an Italian maternity ward)
  carrying the abstract points.

## Verification notes

- The manifest says South Korea is "under 0.8". That held for 2023 (0.72) and 2024 (0.75). The preliminary 2025 figure is 0.80, so the speech says Korea went below 0.8 and has just climbed back to it. Taiwan (0.695 in 2025) is now the lowest reported rate, and the speech says so.
- China's 2025 TFR is not quoted: the figure of about 0.96 that some outlets report could not be traced to the NBS.
- General historical facts used without a web check this session, all standard record: Pericles spoke in the first winter of the Peloponnesian War (431 BC) and the plague followed (the plague's timing is confirmed by the next chapter heading in the Crawley text); the Gettysburg Address was given on 19 November 1863 after Edward Everett's two-hour oration, and runs fewer than three hundred words. Also from the standard historical record: Pericles died within a few years of the oration; the one-child policy was enforced with fines, forced abortions and sterilisations, and was replaced by two- and then three-child limits; South Korea ran family-planning campaigns for smaller families from the 1960s to the 1980s; Romania's 1966 ban on most abortion and contraception led to deaths from illegal abortions and to abandoned children in orphanages; the US baby boom followed the Depression and WWII. These are described in general terms, with no specific figures.
- The web-search budget ran out partway through research. Some figures I had planned (Korea's super-aged threshold, Japan's share of people over 65, global annual births peaking) were left out instead of quoted from memory.
