## Thesis

Americans say they want more children than they are having. A large part of
that gap is not a change of heart but a change of prices: housing, childcare,
debt, delayed adulthood and a tax and regulatory code that, piece by piece,
priced the family out. The strongest counterargument (fertility has fallen
even where policy is generous, and economists find "shifting priorities" rather
than prices) is stated in full and answered: priorities are formed under
prices; the goal is freedom to have the family people say they want, not a
national quota; and honesty about what money can and cannot do.

What people want

- Wendy Wang and Brad Wilcox, IFS (Aug. 15, 2024): expected lifetime births about 1.6; desired slightly over two; IFS/YouGov 2024 women under 35 (30% have children, 41% want, 15% unsure, 14% don't); IFS/Wheatley survey of women whose desired fertility exceeds actual: 36% cite no suitable partner, 36% cannot afford. https://ifstudies.org/blog/women-want-more-children-than-theyre-having-america-can-do-more-to-help

- Pew Research Center (July 2024), via Axios: 47% of childless adults under 50 say unlikely to have children (up from 37% in 2018); of those, 57% just don't want to, 44% want to focus on other things, 38% state of the world, 36% can't afford. https://www.axios.com/2024/07/25/adults-no-children-why-pew-data

Housing
