## Thesis

Women's entry into careers is one of the great achievements of the last century and must be defended. But the country rebuilt its economy, its housing market and its working hours around two full careers per household, and stopped making room for children. The years of career building and the years of peak fertility now overlap, and the costs of a child fall heavily on mothers (time, pay, promotion). The remedy is not to roll back women's choices or to coerce anyone, but to make family economically and culturally viable: paid leave, childcare, family-scaled pay and housing, redesigned work, and fathers who carry half.

Fertility and births

- CDC FastStats, births (final 2024 data): 3,628,934 births; fertility rate 53.8. https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/births.htm
- Total fertility rate series (NCHS figures as tabulated on Wikipedia, "Demographics of the United States"): 3.767 in 1957 (peak), 2.120 in 2007, 1.641 in 2020, 1.621 in 2023, 1.599 in 2024 (final, lowest on record), 1.583 in 2025 (provisional). The 2025 provisional NCHS report itself does not publish a TFR, so the speech calls the 2025 figure "provisional" and "about one point five eight." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_United_States

- Pew Research Center, "The Experiences of U.S. Adults Who Don't Have Children," July 25, 2024: 47% of non-parents under 50 say unlikely ever to have kids, up from 37% in 2018; among them 57% "just don't want to" (64% of women, 50% of men), 44% want to focus on other things such as career, 38% state of the world, 36% can't afford, 24% haven't found the right partner. https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2024/07/25/the-experiences-of-u-s-adults-who-dont-have-children/

Women's work and Claudia Goldin
- Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences 2023 to Claudia Goldin "for having advanced our understanding of women's labour market outcomes"; first woman to win alone. - Goldin, Career and Family: Women's Century-Long Journey toward Equity (Princeton University Press, 2021): generations of college-educated women; greedy work; couple equity; COVID and flexible work. https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691201788/career-and-family
- Goldin, "Babies and the Macroeconomy," NBER Working Paper 33311 (December 2024, rev. Child penalties and the cost of children
- Kleven, Landais and Søgaard, "Children and Gender Inequality: Evidence from Denmark," NBER w24219 / AEJ: Applied 2019: long-run child penalty about 20% of earnings; share of gender inequality due to child penalties rose from about 40% (1980) to about 80% (2013); transmitted from mothers to daughters. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24219
- Kleven, Landais, Posch, Steinhauer and Zweimüller, "Child Penalties across Countries," AEA Papers and Proceedings 2019: long-run penalties of 31% (US) and 44% (UK) (figures as reported in search summaries of the paper; the LSE PDF could not be loaded directly).
- Michelle Budig, "The Fatherhood Bonus and the Motherhood Penalty," Third Way, September 2014: about 4% lower earnings per child for mothers; over 6% more for fathers. https://www.thirdway.org/report/the-fatherhood-bonus-and-the-motherhood-penalty-parenthood-and-the-gender-gap-in-pay
- Correll, Benard and Paik, "Getting a Job: Is There a Motherhood Penalty?" American Journal of Sociology 2007 (laboratory study with résumés): 84% of childless women vs 47% of mothers recommended for hire; mothers offered about $11,000 less. Summary via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherhood_penalty
- Child Care Aware of America, 2024 price landscape: national average price $13,128; about 10% of median married-couple income, about 35% for single parents; center care for two children exceeds median rent in 49 states plus DC. https://www.childcareaware.org/price-landscape24/
- Warren and Tyagi, The Two-Income Trap (Basic Books; first published 2003, reissued 2016): two-earner families less secure; housing in good school districts; the at-home parent as "the most important part of the safety net"; critique by Matt Bruenig. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Two-Income_Trap

The new economics of fertility
- Doepke, Hannusch, Kindermann and Tertilt, "The Economics of Fertility: A New Era," Handbook of the Economics of the Family (2023); IZA DP 15224 / NBER w29948 / Northwestern IPR WP-22-18. Across 22 OECD countries, the correlation between TFR and female labor force participation turned from negative in the 1970s to positive from the 1980s on; four factors that make career and family compatible: family policy, cooperative fathers, favorable norms, flexible labor markets; fathers' share of childcare ranges from under 25% (Russia) to over 40% (Norway); the five countries with the highest male share of housework and childcare all have TFR of 1.8 or higher, the five lowest all below 1.5, correlation above 0.7; East German women return to work faster than West German women even after moving West. https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/our-work/working-papers/2022/wp-22-18.html (PDF text extracted locally)

Biology
