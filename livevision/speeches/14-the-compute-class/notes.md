## The rhetorical model and the inversion

Napoleon's first proclamation has three moves: the hardship named plainly
(you are ragged, hungry, unpaid), the acknowledgment (your patience and
courage have won you nothing), and the promise (I will lead you to the
richest plains in the world, where honor, glory and riches wait). His April
26 proclamation adds the roll-call of victories, the question "is anyone's
courage failing?", and a condition: respect the people you pass through;
looters will be shot.

The inversion used here:

1. The speaker addresses the ragged army, but the abundance is not on a
   foreign plain to be seized. It already exists, at home, built in large
   part from public investment and from the work and words of the very
   people now told they are surplus.
2. The road to it is not conquest but terms: ownership, bargaining, tax,
   public infrastructure. The weapon is institutions, not bayonets.
3. Napoleon's promised riches were someone else's (Italy paid for the
   campaign). The speech refuses that logic: the goal is not to plunder the
   compute class but to stop the abundance from being enclosed.
4. The April "condition" (respect the inhabitants, no looting) is turned
   around and addressed to the compute class itself in section XII.

Techniques used: anaphora ("You have..."; "The plains are..."), tricolon,
antithesis (ragged/rich, wield/replaced, own/owned), direct second-person
address to two audiences, concrete stories (Medvi, Base44, the Luddite
trials at York, the Nice mutiny), a roll-call build, and a peroration that
reprises the opening proclamation in inverted form.

History and policy
- Luddites: began Arnold, Nottinghamshire, 11 March 1811; Frame Breaking
  Act 1812 made machine-breaking a capital crime; Hobsbawm: about 12,000
  troops, more than Wellington took to Portugal in 1808; York trials Jan
  1813, over 60 charged, 30 acquitted; executions and transportation; not
  opposed to machines per se: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddite
- Keynes popularised "technological unemployment" as a temporary phase of
  maladjustment: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment
- Sam Altman, "Moore's Law for Everything" (16 March 2021): American Equity
  Fund, 2.5% annual levy on large companies (in shares) and land; estimated
  about $13,500 per adult per year after a decade; "Even more power will
  shift from labor to capital": https://moores.samaltman.com/
