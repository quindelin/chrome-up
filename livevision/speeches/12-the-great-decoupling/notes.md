## Thesis

Pay tracked productivity for a generation after the Second World War, then split from it. Labor's share of income is now the lowest BLS has ever recorded. The AI buildout, by one Brookings estimate larger as a share of GDP than the railroad boom, is financed and owned by a narrow slice of the country, and the early labor evidence shows AI substituting for entry-level workers. Unless we change the rules (taxes, ownership, bargaining, antitrust), the returns will flow to the owners of models, chips and data, and the gap becomes a chasm. The bargain was built once by law and can be built again.

## Rhetorical techniques borrowed (not copied)

- Bryan: the antithesis of "you tell us / we reply"; widening who counts ("the wage earner is as much a stakeholder as the investor"); two theories of prosperity (trickle down vs build up from below); the closing double "you shall not".
- FDR: naming a new concentration of private power as a political problem of freedom; "political freedom without economic freedom"; the historical arc from one tyranny to the next; "rendezvous with destiny"-style generational charge.
- Both: concrete story carrying the abstraction, anaphora, tricolon, a build to the peroration.
- Short public-domain quotations used: Bryan "their prosperity will leak through on those below"; FDR "privileged enterprise, not free enterprise" and "Necessitous men are not free men" (FDR quoting an English judge).
