## How the Cooper Union model is used

At Cooper Union Lincoln took his opponent's own premise (Douglas: the
founders "understood this question just as well, and even better, than we
do now") as the agreed text, and then counted, signer by signer and vote by
vote, until the evidence reached a conclusion the opponent could not escape
(21 of the 39 signers acted for federal control of slavery in the
territories). Only after the evidence did he turn, in direct address, first
to the other side and then to his own, and end on a moral charge.

This speech does the same thing:

1. It takes the optimists' premise as the agreed text: *technology has
   always created more work than it destroyed*. It grants it.
2. It then calls witnesses one at a time (the graduates, the payroll, the
   firms, and four occupations), stating each figure with its source and
   its caveat, the way Lincoln corrected his own count from 30 appearances
   down to 23 distinct signers.
3. It states the strongest objection at full strength and answers it.
4. It turns to direct address: first to employers, then to government and
   to "those of us who are already on the ladder".
5. Peroration to the young, ending on Lincoln's charge (quoted once and
   attributed, public domain).

Cooper Union techniques borrowed (not sentences): the conceded premise,
the patient count, self-correction of one's own numbers, the repeated
question to the opponent ("what would you have us do?" becomes "who will be
senior in 2040?"), the shift from evidence to duty, and the plain closing
charge.
