- Estimated runtime at 140 wpm: about 117 minutes (1 hour 57 minutes)

## Thesis

America is in a friendship recession: a measurable, two-decade contraction in the time we
spend with one another, the institutions that used to bring us together, and the number of
people who have someone to call. It is a public health problem, not just a private sadness,
and like any recession it has causes that are partly structural and therefore partly fixable
by policy and institutions, not only by individual willpower. Rebuilding requires social
infrastructure, pro-connection policy and a personal ethic of the standing invitation.

## Refrains and devices

- Pericles: "we regard the one who takes no part ... not as unambitious but as useless"
  (Crawley, public domain) is quoted once and answered; "acquiring our friends by conferring,
  not by receiving, favours" quoted once. Praise of what a city can be, then the turn to
  what ours has become.
- Douglass-style refrains in my own words: "Somebody used to be there." (the losses),
  "Count the chairs." (the ledger), and in the peroration "Set the table."
- The opening story is explicitly framed as an imagined composite, not a real person.

## Sources (all checked October 2026 unless noted)

1. U.S. Surgeon General, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation* (advisory), released
   May 2, 2023 (dated May 3 on HHS). Figures used as reported in coverage of the advisory:
   about one in two U.S. adults report loneliness; mortality impact comparable to smoking up
   to fifteen cigarettes a day; loneliness raises risk of premature death about 26 percent,

   70 percent (about 150 to 40 minutes a day); six pillars of a national strategy (social

   "poured fuel on a fire that was already burning" (paraphrased in speech, not quoted).
