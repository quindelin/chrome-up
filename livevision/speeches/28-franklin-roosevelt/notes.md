# Franklin D. Roosevelt: Speeches (notes)

Kind: transcription. All nine texts are public addresses by a sitting US
President, so they are US government works in the public domain. Original
English, so no translator is involved.

## Length and runtime

- Spoken words, not counting headings, context notes or cues: **16,993**
- `wc -w speech.md`, which counts everything: 17,229
- Estimated runtime at 140 wpm: **about 121 minutes (2 hours 1 minute)**

The seven speeches named in the manifest come to 13,856 words (about 99
minutes). To reach two hours I added two more complete public-domain FDR
speeches, the Second Inaugural (1937) and the Third Inaugural (1941). Each is
included in full, with nothing excerpted or padded.

The figures above are reading-speed estimates, not FDR's own delivery times.
He often spoke more slowly than 140 wpm, and the convention and congressional
speeches stopped for applause, so the real delivery times were longer.

## Editorial changes (the words themselves are unchanged)

- Added: the speech headings, a one-line italic context note per speech, and
  a few delivery cues on their own lines ([LET IT LAND], [PAUSE], [BUILD],
  [SLOW]).
- Long source paragraphs are split at sentence boundaries into chunks of
  about three sentences so they read better on the HUD. No words are added,
  removed or reordered. A script checked that the speech text has the same
  16,993-word sequence as the extracted source.
- Removed APP editorial material that is not part of the speech: page
  titles, "[See APP Note.]", "[First Fireside Chat]", APP notes, keyword
  lines, citation lines, and the inline "[See APP note]" after "March 3d"
  in the banking chat. The original spelling "March 3d" is kept.
- Fixed one APP transcription typo in the inaugural: "And]I" is now "And I".
- Spelling and punctuation follow APP as published, including "Teheran",
  "disaster- that" and "a sure peace a peace invulnerable".

## Not verified

- I did not check the texts against a second archive (FDR Library, National
  Archives or Miller Center). WebSearch was unavailable, and APP is one of
  the sources the brief approves.
- The context notes use only the dates and places APP gives. Statements such
  as "eight days after the inauguration" and "as Allied forces landed in
  Normandy" follow from those dates and from the texts themselves.
