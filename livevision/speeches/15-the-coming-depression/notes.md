## Thesis

Artificial intelligence threatens a demand-side depression for working people:
when wages vanish from the bottom and middle of the income ladder, the
spending that makes up two-thirds of the economy goes with them. The economy
already stands on one leg (the top ten percent), households are carrying record
nominal debt, housing has slipped out of reach, and history (1873, 1929, the
Luddites and Engels' pause, Youngstown and the China shock) shows that the
"long run" in which technology pays off for everyone can take a generation and
can pass through catastrophe on the way. The speech is a warning, not a claim
that the depression has already arrived: it says plainly that unemployment is
4.2 percent and GDP is still growing.

## Rhetorical techniques borrowed (in my own words)

- Demosthenes: the opening complaint that much is said and nothing is done;
  "if we were all agreed that we are at war" turned into "if we were all agreed
  that the alarm is real"; the litany of the adversary's advances, each one
  answered by delay; the contrast of ancestors and present; the "disease
  that is already in the body" image; the concrete program of action at the end
  rather than mere exhortation.
- Churchill-style escalation: each section raises the stakes, short declarative
  sentences after long ones, antithesis ("not the machine, but the bargain"),
  anaphora in the build to the peroration.
- Direct address to the audience; concrete stories (a 22-year-old graduate, a
  stockinger in Nottinghamshire, a steelworker on Black Monday) carrying the
  abstract argument.

History
- Panic of 1873 (Wikipedia): Jay Cooke & Company failed September 18, 1873 over Northern Pacific bonds; NYSE closed ten days from September 20; 55 railroads failed by November 1873, 60 more within a year; 18,000 business failures 1873-75; track construction fell from 7,500 miles (1872) to 1,600 (1875); Coinage Act of 1873. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1873
- NBER business cycle dates: contraction October 1873 to March 1879, 65 months, longest on record; August 1929 to March 1933, 43 months. https://www.nber.org/research/data/us-business-cycle-expansions-and-contractions
- Great Railroad Strike of 1877 (Wikipedia): July 14 to September 4; B&O third wage cut in a year, 10%; Pittsburgh roundhouse; Hayes sent federal troops; an estimated 126 to 160 killed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Railroad_Strike_of_1877
- Great Depression in the United States (Wikipedia): Lebergott's 24.9% unemployment estimate for 1933; over 9,000 banks closed; corn from $1.43 a bushel (1929) to $0.31 (1932); Hoovervilles. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States
- Roaring Twenties (Wikipedia): US electricity production almost quadrupled; about 15 million Model Ts (1908-1927); farming and coal mining stagnated. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Twenties
- Luddite (Wikipedia): began Arnold, Nottinghamshire, March 11, 1811; targeted employers using machines to undercut wages and practices; about 12,000 troops (Hobsbawm: more than Wellington took to Portugal in 1808); Frame Breaking Act 1812 made machine breaking a capital crime; York trials January 1813; Byron in the Lords, February 27, 1812. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddite
- Engels' pause (Wikipedia, after Robert C. Allen): wages stagnated 1790-1840 while output per head grew; labour share about 50% (1801) to about 45% (1845); disputed by Gregory Clark and others. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engels%27_pause
- FRED MANEMP: manufacturing employment 19,553,000 (June 1979 peak); 17,284,000 (Jan 2000); 11,447,000 (Jan 2010); 12,652,000 (Sept 2026).
- Youngstown Sheet and Tube (Wikipedia): Black Monday, September 19, 1977, Campbell Works closed, 5,000 workers furloughed; Brier Hill closed 1979. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown_Sheet_and_Tube
- China shock (Wikipedia): WTO accession 2001; Acemoglu et al. (2016) estimate 2.0-2.4 million jobs; exposed regions saw higher unemployment, lower participation, lower wages. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_shock
- Deaths of despair (Wikipedia): Case and Deaton 2015; about 158,000 deaths in 2018 vs 65,000 in 1995; life expectancy of Americans without a college degree peaked in 2010. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_of_despair

Counterarguments

- Technological unemployment (Wikipedia): lump-of-labour fallacy, compensation effects; Keynes popularised "technological unemployment" around 1930 and called it a temporary phase of maladjustment. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment
