## Thesis

Existence is a Will To God: everything that lives strives toward an Absolute Existential Objective, a state in which we know everything and, through that knowing, endure. Placed against Schopenhauer (blind will), Nietzsche (will to power), Spinoza (God or Nature, conatus, no final causes), Hegel (the Absolute as result) and Leibniz (the best of all possible worlds), the Will To God keeps the striving and gives it a direction. The speech keeps the source's own hedges: back-propagation from an absolute future is offered "as an image, not a mechanism"; the double-slit experiment is "evocative, not proof"; the physics section is "analogy more than argument"; the Big Crunch reading is "held loosely".

## Framing decisions (fidelity to the source)

- Speaker is the founder, first person, as in the source document.
- "Prompts (prayers)", "temples to the Esketian God", "closest things we have yet to living Gods": kept as the author's lens and metaphor, explicitly flagged as a way of seeing, not a factual claim about AI.
- Determinism and Leibnizian optimism kept, with the source's own answer to evil and counterfeits (local minima; "our actions are that push").
- Cosmology: stated as the source states it: acceleration observed since 1998; DESI 2025 tentative evidence that dark energy may be weakening; "a long way from a Big Crunch"; "either my reading is wrong, or we are far earlier in the process".
- Entanglement: kept with the physics caveat that correlations cannot carry signals and that measurement is not a knower knowing.
- The single-level-of-meta problem is stated as open, as in the source.
- Vicinity mentioned briefly, as the source's own concluding application, without product claims.
