## Sources (all public domain; English originals, no translator)

1. **Newburgh Address**: Wikisource, "Newburgh address"
   https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Newburgh_address
   (the text follows Fitzpatrick, *The Writings of George Washington*, a US government publication, 1931-44).
2. **Circular to the States**: Wikisource, "Circular to the States"
   https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Circular_to_the_States
   (from Fitzpatrick, *Writings*, vol. 26, original spelling kept). Checked word by word against the
   second Wikisource edition, "A Circular Letter from his Excellency George Washington"
   (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Circular_Letter_from_his_Excellency_George_Washington), which is dated
   June 18, 1783 and uses modernized spelling. The two differ only in spelling and minor wording.

   https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/wash1.asp

5. **Second Inaugural Address**: Avalon Project

6. **Farewell Address**: Avalon Project
   https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp

National Archives Founders Online was tried first, but it served a bot

against it.

## Editorial changes (transcription and whitespace fixes only, no change to the wording)

- Newburgh: the source marks the word "designing" as struck through
  ("Some ~~designing~~ emissary"), meaning Washington crossed it out in his manuscript. It is
  left out of the script. Also "measures?." was changed to "measures?".
- Circular: "As to file second Article" was changed to "As to the second Article". This is an OCR error;
  the second edition has "the".
- Farewell: "April, I793" was changed to "April, 1793" (OCR), "virtue ?" to "virtue?", and Avalon's
  " - It will be worthy" to "— It will be worthy". Two paragraphs that the Avalon page broke
  mid-sentence were joined back together ("modified by mutual interests" and "as we are now at liberty").
- First Inaugural: "fellow- citizens" was changed to "fellow-citizens" (hyphenation artifact). The source's
  "the motives which mislead me" is kept as printed.
- Runs of double spaces were collapsed.
- Added: a speech heading, a one-line italic context note per speech, and a delivery cue at the end of
  each section.

## Not included or unverified

- Washington's well-known unscripted remark at Newburgh, when he put on his spectacles, is not part of the
  written address in these sources. It is left out because no verbatim public-domain text was fetched for it.
- The Circular's closing signature line ("I have the honor to be...") appears only in the second edition,
  not in the Fitzpatrick-based text used here, so it is left out.
- Facts in the context notes (Temple at the New Windsor cantonment; Maryland State House; Federal Hall;
  Congress Hall; first printing in the *American Daily Advertiser* on September 19, 1796; the Farewell Address
  was never delivered aloud) are standard history. WebSearch was not used to re-check them this session.
